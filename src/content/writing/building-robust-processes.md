I recently spoke to a top portfolio manager at Citadel.

He and his team of 3 investors manage over $1B in public equities and have done very well in a volatile year where [many hedge funds have struggled](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-11-06/tech-selloff-wipes-out-hedge-fund-gains-prompting-risk-cuts-and).

Naturally, I asked him what he attributed his success to. You’d expect his answer to be a cliché:

- ‘We work 6 days a week and put in over 80 hours every week’
- ‘We encourage debate and maximize truth seeking’
- ‘We foster an environment of high intellectual safety’

Instead, he said: ‘I attribute the vast majority of our team’s success to **building robust processes.**’

Dumbfounded, I asked a string of follow-on questions:

**_What is a robust process?_**\
A hyperstreamlined set of steps that achieves a specific goal.

**_How do you build a robust process?_**\
Start by building a (not robust) process. What is your ultimate goal? What are the discrete steps required to achieve that goal? Then, scrutinize each step, understand their nuances, and make adjustments to make the process robust. Can it be done better? Can it be done faster? Can it be done more efficiently?

**_Why do robust processes work so well?_**

1. Forces progress towards the ultimate goal on day one (even if processes aren’t perfect)
2. Ensures energy is concentrated in the right places over time to drive meaningful impact
3. Creates a compounding effect as steps are repeatedly being scrutinized and improved

These answers aren’t glamorous. In fact, they are quite simple.

So I challenge you to take this wisdom and apply it to your own life. What are your goals? What steps should you follow to achieve these goals? How can each of these steps get better, faster, more efficient, etc. over time?
